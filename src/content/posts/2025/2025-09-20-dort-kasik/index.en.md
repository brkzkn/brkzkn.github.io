A six-table mantı house on a Çengelköy back street. The dumplings are folded at the front window and the yoghurt is made in the back. Fifty minutes at a table with the twins, our longest sit-down so far.

## Facts

- Went with | All four of us, the twins in the pram beside the table
- Table | By the window, next to the folding counter
- Booking | None, you wait at the door

## What we ordered

Four things for two adults, and a spoon each for the other two.

- **Kayseri mantı**: Small and folded tight. Garlic yoghurt underneath, browned butter with red pepper on top. We asked for half the yoghurt and extra sumac, and that is how I would order it again.
- **Yüksük soup**: Thimble-sized dumplings and chickpeas in a sour tomato broth, with dried mint. The better dish for a cool day, and the one the twins ate most of.
- **Fried mantı**: Crisp, with the yoghurt on the side. Good for the first five and heavy after that. One plate is enough for a table.
- **Shepherd’s salad**: Cut small and served cold, with more lemon than oil. Something sharp between the boiled mantı and the fried.

The plates came within ten minutes, and they did not charge for the tea afterwards. The woman at the folding counter waved at the pram for most of the meal.

## The verdict

Dört Kaşık does one thing. The menu is a single laminated page: three kinds of mantı, two soups, a salad and ayran. Everything is made in the room you are sitting in, and you can watch the dough being rolled and cut while you wait. The mantı is the small Kayseri kind, where a spoon holds six, and it arrives hot enough that the yoghurt has not cooled it down yet.

It loses a star for the room, not the food. Six tables is too few on a Saturday, the door opens straight onto the queue, and the only place for a double pram is half in the aisle. The staff were kind about it, but we ate faster than the food deserved.

## Would I go again?

Yes, on a weekday, and next time I am not sharing the soup.
