Nine tables under a vine on a Kuzguncuk side street. The courgette flowers were the plate of the night, and the next table was forty centimetres away.

## What we ordered

Six plates for two, shared, in the order the kitchen sent them.

- **Stuffed courgette flowers**: Eight to a plate, served just warm. Rice, dill and a little mint, and thin enough that you taste the flower. We ordered a second plate.
- **Samphire with garlic and olive oil**: Blanched for a minute at most, still crisp. The olive oil is from Ayvalık and they bring the bottle if you ask.
- **Artichoke hearts with broad beans**: Good, but late in the season for it and a touch soft. The lemon carried it.
- **Grilled octopus**: One thick arm, charred outside and tender through. Served on fava, which it did not need.
- **Sea bream, grilled whole**: Filleted at the table in under a minute. Crisp skin, nothing on it but salt, oil and oregano.
- **Mastic pudding with sour cherry**: The weak plate. Set too firm and sweeter than everything before it. We left half.

Service was quick and unhurried at the same time. Bread and water arrived before we sat down properly, and nobody asked whether we were finished.

## The verdict

Asma Altı does a short menu and does most of it well. The cold olive-oil dishes are the reason to come: they taste of the vegetable first and the oil second, and they arrive at the temperature they should be eaten at, which is rarer than it sounds. The fish is simple and correctly cooked. The courtyard is the other half of it, with a vine that still had its leaves in the first week of November and a string of bulbs low enough to light the plates.

The half point comes off for two things. The dessert was not at the level of the rest, and the tables are so close together that by nine o’clock we knew the neighbours’ holiday plans in detail. On a Thursday the courtyard was full and loud. We had two and a half hours without a pram in sight, so we did not mind much.

## Would I go again?

Yes. Early sitting, a corner table, and no dessert.
